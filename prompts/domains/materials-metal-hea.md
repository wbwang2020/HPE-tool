# Domain card: metals and high-entropy alloys (materials)

Applies to metals, alloys, high-entropy alloys (HEA), coatings, structural materials and processing–structure–property studies. The six HPE dimensions remain the scoring frame; map them as follows: technical_soundness = thermodynamics/kinetics/mechanism and processing logic; evidence_strength = characterization and mechanical-test evidence; reproducibility = composition/processing/characterization traceability; significance = property/mechanism advance.

## Required chain of evidence

A materials claim should connect:

**composition / feedstock → processing route → microstructure (phase, grain, texture, defects) → property → performance/service behavior**

Every link needs evidence. A property number without processing and microstructure context is not interpretable.

## What counts as evidence

- **Composition**: at.%/wt.% clearly stated, impurity/oxygen/nitrogen level, weighing/melting route, homogeneity evidence.
- **Processing**: melting/sintering/rolling/annealing/forging parameters with time, temperature, atmosphere, cooling rate; reproducibility across batches.
- **Microstructure**: XRD phase identification (with reference cards/PDF), SEM/TEM/EBSD images with scale bars, grain size, phase fraction, texture, dislocation/defect evidence; not only one micrograph.
- **Mechanical**: specimen geometry, standard (ASTM/ISO/GB), strain rate, temperature, number of specimens, mean ± SD, fracture surfaces; hardness vs tension vs compression distinguished.
- **Thermodynamics/kinetics**: CALPHAD parameters, phase stability, segregation, diffusion, validation against experiment.
- **Corrosion/oxidation**: environment, electrolyte, exposure time, standard, replicate; not a single polarization curve.
- **Data provenance**: raw curves, supplementary tables, code/analysis scripts, sample IDs linking micrograph to test.

## Common loopholes

1. **Composition–property shortcut**: claims a composition effect without controlling processing or microstructure.
2. **Single-sample / single-batch**: one tensile curve, one micrograph, one XRD pattern; no repeatability or batch variance.
3. **Phase misidentification**: peak indexing without reference cards, minor phases below detection, oxide/carbide mislabeled.
4. **Processing confound**: annealing time and temperature changed together; cooling rate differs across samples.
5. **Specimen/test artifact**: size effect, surface finish, grip alignment, machine compliance, strain measurement method.
6. **Cherry-picked region**: EBSD/SEM area not representative; grain size from a favorable region.
7. **Thermodynamic inconsistency**: CALPHAD prediction not validated; phase diagram read at wrong temperature/composition.
8. **Property inflation**: hardness converted to strength without a validated relation; best sample reported as typical.
9. **Corrosion/oxidation overclaim**: short exposure, non-standard electrolyte, no replicate, no post-test characterization.
10. **Missing statistics**: no error bars, no number of specimens, no outlier policy; "excellent" without comparison to established alloys.

## Review focus

- Separate **innovation** (new composition/mechanism/processing route) from **technical highlight** (new characterization or processing insight).
- For every major issue, cite the exact figure/table/page and state the missing control, replicate, standard or characterization.
- Do not demand ML/benchmark-style statistics where the field uses standard specimens; do demand specimen count, scatter and test standard.
- For a review paper, assess coverage of alloy systems, processing routes and mechanisms, not "no new alloy".
