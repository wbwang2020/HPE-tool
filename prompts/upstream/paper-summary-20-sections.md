1. Basic information: authors, year, title, venue, volume/pages, DOI.
2. (Focus) The core scientific problem or technical bottleneck, and the authors' stated motivation. For a survey/review, analyze the heuristic narrative that organizes the field.
3. The core research hypotheses or theoretical constructs the authors explicitly propose.
4. (Long-form, do not skip) Research design: if a research paper, at least 1500 Chinese characters / about 900 English words explaining the overall model architecture and the derivation strategy; explain the meaning of core equations and how each lemma/theorem/corollary is formed. If a survey, analyze the evolution of the method families and the organization logic. Use `$...$` for inline and `$$...$$` for display formulas; give 1–2 sentences on the physical/mathematical meaning of each formula.
5. Data/sample sources and reproducibility: acquisition, sample size, selection criteria, features; code/data availability, hyperparameter disclosure, compute.
6. (Long-form, do not skip for non-survey) Methods and technology: at least 1500 Chinese characters / about 900 English words; algorithms, models, platforms, tools, and construction details.
7. Analysis procedure: step-by-step experiment/simulation/derivation; baseline selection and ablation design.
8. Data analysis: statistical methods, validity checks, metrics.
9. Core findings: the most important and novel observations.
10. Experimental results: key quantitative results (metrics, significance) and qualitative conclusions.
11. Supporting results: secondary evidence.
12. Conclusions the authors draw from their evidence.
13. From a demanding reviewer's perspective: theoretical innovation and gaps, technical breakthroughs and limits, concrete contribution to the field and possible defects.
14. Relation to the state of the art: core connections and claimed advantages relative to frontier work.
15. Highlights and weaknesses, with a critical-reviewer emphasis: novelty, controversy, limitations, future implications.
16. All Figures and Tables: number, title, content summary, page, so evidence can be located.
17. Evaluation: your judgment of methodological rigor, logical completeness and reliability.
18. Questions and gaps found while reading.
19. Implications and future directions.
20. One or two representative references (authors, year, title, venue, volume/pages).
